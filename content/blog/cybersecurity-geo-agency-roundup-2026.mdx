A cybersecurity GEO agency optimises a security company's presence for the way large language models generate answers: ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews synthesising a response from several retrieved sources at the moment a buyer asks a question, rather than returning a list of links. Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) is the term researchers coined for this discipline in 2024, and it has since become the industry label for the work, alongside the older term AEO.

This roundup covers what GEO involves, how it differs from AEO in practice, and which agencies in our directory offer it. We name no leader: compare the listings yourself.

## What GEO Involves

GEO work has four parts, and cybersecurity buyers make all four harder than a typical B2B category.

**Entity clarity.** A model needs a consistent, unambiguous picture of what your product does and what category it competes in before it can cite you correctly. Security products often sit across overlapping categories (EDR, XDR, SIEM), which makes this harder to get right than for a single-category SaaS product.

**Source diversity.** Generative models draw from multiple sources when they build an answer, not one. Being present on your own site is not enough. You need presence across the directories, review platforms, and community discussion a model retrieves from for security queries specifically.

**Synthesis-ready content.** Content written to be quoted whole, rather than summarised, gets misrepresented less often when a model blends several sources into one answer. Direct claims, defined terms, and comparison data survive synthesis better than narrative marketing copy.

**Citation monitoring.** Because the model generates a fresh answer each time rather than serving a static page, the only way to know your visibility is to keep asking the models the questions your buyers ask and logging what comes back.

## How GEO Differs from AEO in Practice

The terms overlap enough that most agencies use them interchangeably, and for a buyer evaluating a partner, the distinction rarely changes what to ask for. Where they diverge: AEO has roots in featured snippets and voice-assistant answer boxes, a single authoritative answer to a single question. GEO is specifically about generative synthesis, where a model blends multiple sources into one response, which is why source diversity and citation monitoring matter more here than in classic AEO. ## How to Evaluate a GEO Agency

1. **Ask for the source list, not just the strategy.** A GEO programme should name the specific publications, directories, and platforms it targets for citations in your category, not describe the method in the abstract.
2. **Ask how they monitor synthesis, not just rankings.** Because a generative answer is assembled fresh each time, the agency needs a repeatable way to query the models and log what they generate, on a schedule.
3. **Ask for a security-specific accuracy example.** A model that misattributes a technical claim in security is a bigger problem than a wrong fact about a consumer product. Ask how the agency has handled a citation that got the facts wrong.
4. **Set a realistic timeline.** Citations typically begin appearing in 4 to 12 weeks once entity structure and citations are in place, compounding as authority builds. Nobody delivers generative dominance in two weeks.

## FAQ

**Is GEO the same as AEO?**
They overlap heavily and most agencies use the terms interchangeably. GEO specifically concerns generative synthesis, where a model blends several sources into one answer; AEO's roots are in single-answer extraction, like featured snippets. In practice, ask the same questions of a provider either way.

**How is GEO measured?**
Pick the prompts your buyers actually use, run them monthly across ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and Gemini, and record whether your brand appears, how it is described, and whether the description is accurate. Citation rate on your priority prompts is the core metric.

**Does GEO replace SEO?**
No. The authority and content that earn Google rankings are largely the same signals generative models retrieve from. GEO adds entity work, source diversification, and generative-specific monitoring on top of a working SEO foundation, not instead of one.
